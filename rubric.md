# Entrepreneurship Reflection Rubric

Students record a 3–5 minute video reflecting on the course so far (readings,
lectures, guest speakers, in-class and field activities on entrepreneurship,
problem identification, empathy, user-centered design, product-market fit,
lean startup, etc.).

The reflection is worth 40 points. The professor's target is a class average
of about 88%, so the bar is: **did the student genuinely think it through, in
their own words, with specifics?** This isn't meant to be harsh. A strong,
personal reflection that covers every prompt still earns full marks.

## How to judge each check

Give each of the five checks below one status:

- **thoughtful**: Clearly meets the check, in the student's own words.
- **thin**: Touched on, but vague, generic, or only implied.
- **missing**: Not addressed at all.

Each thin or missing check costs 2 points.

## The five checks

1. **Change in perception**: How their view of entrepreneurship or the
   entrepreneurial process has changed. Thoughtful if they contrast what
   they used to think with what they think now.
2. **Specific takeaway from readings / class material**: They must point to a
   specific reading, case, framework, or class session that can be identified
   (e.g., "the Jobs to Be Done reading", "the milkshake example", "Value
   Proposition Design", "Applegate", "the Zipcar case", "the design brief")
   and explain what they took from it. Course ideas with no identifiable
   source ("empathy is important", "start with the problem") are **thin**.
3. **Experiential learning**: What they got out of a hands-on or in-class
   experience: an exercise, empathy/customer interviews, fieldwork, a guest
   speaker, team venture work. Thoughtful if they describe the experience and
   what it taught them. A single fact recalled with no reflection is thin.
4. **Next-phase priorities for their venture**: They must state what they'll
   prioritize next **for their venture or team project**. A general mindset
   ("I'll listen first", "focus more on execution") that isn't tied to a
   venture is **thin**. So is describing the venture without saying what
   comes next, or naming a tool without saying what they'll do with it.
5. **Personal & specific** (authenticity): Does the student's own experience
   come through: their interviews, their venture, their background, moments
   from class? **Thin** if it reads like a polished, generic summary of course
   ideas that anyone could have written without being in the class. Never
   mark this one missing.

## Possible AI-written script (flag only, never deducted)

Set `possible_ai_script` when there are strong signs the student read an
AI-written script, and quote the signs. Signs include: essay-like polish with
no personal detail; stock AI phrasing such as "curious enough to…, empathetic
enough to…, disciplined enough to…", "it's not X, it's Y" closers, and tidy
three-part lists; summaries that track course material but never mention the
student's own work. Reading from notes the student wrote themselves is not a
sign (it sounds meandering, e.g. "oh yeah, this is the one"). This flag is a
prompt for the TA to watch the video, not an accusation, and doesn't change
the score. Score generic content through check 5 instead.

## Calibration examples

- **Student A** (travel bundles venture, joined the class late): perception,
  experiential (empathy interviews, asking "why" repeatedly), next phase
  (talk to college students about price and convenience pain points), and
  personal are **thoughtful**. Readings are **thin**: he discusses empathy
  interviewing but never names the reading or material it came from. (The TA
  gave 40 before the stricter rule.)
- **Student B** (travel bundle website): perception, readings (Jobs to Be
  Done, milkshake → Poppi), experiential (Lumio guest speakers), and personal
  are **thoughtful**. Next phase is **thin**: they describe the venture but
  never say what they'll prioritize next.
- **Student C** (Priya gave 38): perception, readings (Applegate, named and
  explained), experiential (his own interviews, five whys, Lumio), and personal
  are **thoughtful**. Next phase is **thin**: he wants to move from interviews
  to building, but says nothing about how, or how he'll test viability. 38.
- **Student D** (Priya gave 38): perception, experiential (interviews,
  venture spotlights, specific Lumio / Y Combinator lessons), and personal are
  **thoughtful**. Readings are **thin** ("a lot of readings we've done", none
  named). Next phase is **missing**. 36.
- **Generic example**: a reflection that summarizes the Zipcar and Twitter
  cases and the course framework well, but never mentions an interview, their
  venture, or anything from their own experience: readings **thoughtful**,
  personal **thin**.

## Notes for the grader

- Transcripts are machine-generated; ignore filler words, mis-transcriptions,
  and odd names (e.g., "Milk Sheik" = milkshake, a guest company's name may be misspelled).
- Whisper sometimes invents text when a video ends in silence (a phrase
  repeated over and over, or "you. you. you."). Ignore it when judging, and
  add a flag so the TA can check the end of the video.
- Delivery (filler words, volume, reading from notes) is not scored. Mention
  it in the comment as coaching if it stands out.
- Quote short evidence from the transcript for every judgment.
- Students read these comments to understand why they didn't get full marks.
  Write each rationale to the student ("You ..."). For a thin or missing
  check, say exactly what was missing, e.g. "You described your travel
  website, but didn't say what you'll prioritize next for it." Write the
  overall comment as 1-2 sentences of specific praise; deductions are
  explained separately. Never mention the AI-script flag to the student.
- Target length is 3–5 minutes. Flag videos outside that range for the TA,
  but don't let length affect the statuses.
