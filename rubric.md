# Entrepreneurship Reflection Rubric

Students record a 3–5 minute video reflecting on the course so far (readings,
lectures, guest speakers, in-class and field activities on entrepreneurship,
problem identification, empathy, user-centered design, product-market fit,
lean startup, etc.).

This is a low-stakes, generous grade (worth 40 points). The question for each
prompt is simply: **did the student genuinely think it through?** Most
reflections that make an honest effort should earn full marks. Deductions are
for reflections that are clearly thin, not for missing polish or citations.

## How to judge each prompt

Give each of the four prompts one status:

- **thoughtful**: The student engages with it in their own words and connects
  it to their own thinking or experience. It does NOT need to name a specific
  reading, cite an author, follow the prompt order, or be long. When in doubt
  between thoughtful and thin, choose thoughtful.
- **thin**: Touched on, but only in a sentence or two of generic statements
  that show little personal reflection, or implied rather than actually
  said.
- **missing**: Not addressed at all.

## The four prompts

1. **Change in perception**: How their view of entrepreneurship or the
   entrepreneurial process has changed.
2. **Readings / assigned materials**: Takeaways from readings or assigned
   materials. Talking through an idea from the material (e.g., Jobs to Be
   Done, empathy interviewing, "listening beneath the surface", validated
   learning) counts even if the reading isn't named.
3. **Experiential learning**: What they got out of a hands-on or in-class
   experience: an exercise, empathy/customer interviews, fieldwork, a guest
   speaker, team venture work. Reflecting on the practice of interviewing or
   on a guest speaker's story counts.
4. **Next-phase priorities**: What they'll prioritize next in their venture.
   Thoughtful if they state a priority and why, even briefly. Thin if it's only
   implied (e.g., they describe their venture but never say what they'll do
   next).

## Calibration examples (graded by the TA)

- **Student A** (travel bundles venture, joined the class late): full marks. He
  never named a reading and described reading about empathy interviews rather
  than conducting one, but his discussion of empathy interviews, asking "why"
  repeatedly to find root causes, and focusing on student pain points (price,
  convenience) was genuinely thoughtful. All four are **thoughtful**.
- **Student B** (travel bundle website): perception,
  readings (Jobs to Be Done, milkshake → Poppi), and experiential (Lumio guest
  speakers, hands-on venture research) are all **thoughtful**. Next phase is
  **thin**: they describe the venture but never clearly state what they'll
  prioritize next.

## Notes for the grader

- Transcripts are machine-generated; ignore filler words, mis-transcriptions,
  and odd names (e.g., "Milk Sheik" = milkshake, a guest company's name may be misspelled).
- Quote short evidence from the transcript for every judgment.
- Target length is 3–5 minutes. Flag videos outside that range for the TA,
  but don't let length affect the statuses.
